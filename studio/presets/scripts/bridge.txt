The Bridge That Fell for a Song.

In 1831, the town of Broughton was proud of its new iron bridge. Seventy-four soldiers marched across it in step, boots ringing on the deck. The bridge began to sway with every step. A bolt snapped. The deck folded into the river.

Luckily, nobody drowned. Unluckily, the whole town had to walk the long way round for a year.

A child on a swing knows the trick: a small push at the right moment grows into a huge arc. The soldiers were the push. Afterward, armies were told to break step on bridges. The bridge was rebuilt with a sign, but the sign did not mention the song.
